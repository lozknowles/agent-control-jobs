# Research method and limitations

Research preceded the canonical library. The supplied Google share link resolved to the Dataiku landing page. The user then supplied the full guide; all 21 pages were read, and the inconsistent sales page was visually checked. The original PDF is not redistributed. Its title, digest and access date are in sources.json.

We selected a small purposive sample across business work, coding, support, research, knowledge, model operations and physical systems. Sources include first-party engineering accounts, technical APIs and vendor-described cases. These have different evidential strength: an API demonstrates an interface, an illustrative architecture suggests a workload, and a customer story is still a reported claim. None proves Agent Control can perform it.

Each selected published example has a short paraphrase, normalised objective, source URL, access date, organisation, capabilities, likely integrations, autonomy, mutation risk, approval requirement and canonical mapping. Several examples overlap. No random sample, prevalence estimate or exhaustive industry census is claimed. Categories absent from the sample can still appear as maintainer-required qualification jobs, explicitly labelled by provenance.

Mappings are **component-only** unless independent end-to-end evidence proves otherwise. A record-reconciliation fixture does not reproduce an enterprise billing agent. External workflow mutation permissions remain recorded in research even where the mapped canonical case only proposes a change. Missing connectors, credentials and specialist validators are useful gaps, not reasons to rename the workload as supported.

The guide's Process Automation, Worker Augmentation and Enterprise Intelligence patterns organise simple objectives. Its technical-readiness questions inform resource, data, connector, credential and approval checks. Its proprietary implementations are not copied. Fixed arithmetic fixtures are negative controls and component tests; complex agent reasoning needs varied private holdouts and independent scoring.

Counts in COVERAGE.md describe checked-in records, not unique industry workload prevalence. All selected records remain in the denominator, including clinical, physical safety, creative media and autonomous isolation workloads without suitable jobs. No coverage percentage is published. Additional primary sources and counterexamples are welcome via pull requests.
